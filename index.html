Privacy Policy

Solo Traveler — by LimogiAI Solutions
Last updated 3 November 2026

Solo Traveler is a set of tools for travellers: a currency converter, an EMI and loan calculator, a QR code reader, a travel history, a place to keep your payment cards and travel documents, a visa stay counter, packing lists, things to do, a phrase book, a tip and price checker, a world clock and plug guide, a trip journal and an expense tracker. It was built to work without knowing anything about you, and this policy explains exactly what that means.

In short: no account, no ads, no analytics, no tracking. Everything you enter, including saved cards, stays on your phone. Saved cards are encrypted and never backed up, synced or sent anywhere. We do not collect, sell or share personal information.

What the app does not do

  • It does not ask you to create an account or sign in.
  • It does not show ads or contain advertising code.
  • It does not use analytics, tracking or crash-reporting tools.
  • It does not collect, sell or share your personal information.
  • It does not send your card details, expenses, loan figures or scanned codes to us or to anyone else.

Backing up and restoring your data

In Settings you can save a backup file of your app data (trips and expenses, travel history, document details, visa counters, packing lists, journal notes, your trip plan, your medical card and settings) together with your document, journal, receipt and ticket files. The file is made on your phone and handed to your phone's share menu; it goes only where you choose to save or send it. It is not encrypted and contains copies of your documents and any medical details you entered, so keep it somewhere private. Saved cards are never included. Restoring reads a backup file you choose, on your phone only. Delete all data in Settings removes everything the app has stored, including saved cards and photos.

Tickets and boarding passes

In Trip plan you can attach e-tickets, boarding passes and booking confirmations: a PDF or picture you choose, a photo you take, or (on Android) a file you share to Solo Traveler from another app. The file is copied into the app's private storage on your phone. To fill in a flight, the app looks for a boarding pass barcode in the file, or in what the camera sees when you tap "Scan boarding pass"; this is done on your phone, and camera images are not saved. When you show a boarding pass, the app turns your screen brightness up until you close it. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Emergency

Emergency lists emergency phone numbers for each country. Tapping one opens your phone's dialler with the number; the call is made by your phone, not by the app. "Find the embassy" opens a web search in your browser. Your medical card is stored on your phone only, like your other data, and is shown only when you open it.

Reminders (notifications)

If you allow notifications, the app reminds you of visa days left, documents about to expire and items in your trip plan. Reminders are scheduled on your phone by the phone's own notification system; nothing is sent to us or to anyone else. You can turn reminders off in Settings, or turn off notifications for the app in your phone's settings.

Sharing your plan and your travel map

In Trip plan, "Share my plan" prepares a message with your upcoming plan and hands it to your phone's share menu. It is sent only if you choose an app and send it, and only to the people you choose. The Travel map is drawn on your phone from your travel history; "Share" makes a picture of it and hands it to the share menu in the same way.

Home screen widget (Android)

If you add the Solo Traveler widget to your home screen, it shows your home country, the country you're travelling in, their times and an exchange rate. Anyone who can see your home screen can see this. Remove the widget to stop showing it.

Phrase book speech

When you tap the speaker button in the phrase book, the phrase is read aloud by your phone's own text-to-speech voices. Only the phrase text is passed to your phone's speech service; the app sends nothing to us. Depending on your phone's settings, its speech service may use online voices provided by your phone's maker.

Saying a country (microphone)

Country, currency and language searches have a microphone button so you can say a country instead of typing it. The microphone is used only after you tap that button, and only until you stop speaking. Your words are turned into text by your phone's own speech recognition service (Google on Android, Apple on iPhone), which may process them online under that company's privacy policy. The app only receives the text, uses it to search, and does not record, save or send anything you say. You can turn the microphone off for Solo Traveler at any time in your phone's settings; typing still works.

Importing your travel history

You can import your travel history from a spreadsheet file (CSV) that you choose. The file is read on your phone only and is not sent to us or anyone else.

Exporting a trip

You can export a trip's expenses as a PDF or a spreadsheet (CSV) file. The file is made on your phone and handed to your phone's share menu; it goes only where you choose to send or save it (for example your email, Google Drive or Files). The app does not send it to us. Once shared, the copy is subject to the privacy terms of the app or service you sent it to.

Saved cards

If you add a card in My cards, the app saves what you enter: the card number, expiry date and CVV, and optionally the name on the card, a nickname, the bank and notes. This information:

  • is stored encrypted on your phone only. On Android it is encrypted with a key held in the phone's secure hardware (Android Keystore). On iPhone it is kept in the iOS Keychain, marked for this device only;
  • is never backed up or synced. The app turns off Android backup (Google cloud backup and transfer to a new phone), and on iPhone the card data is not included in iCloud Keychain and cannot be restored onto another device. If you lose or replace your phone, your saved cards cannot be recovered;
  • is never sent anywhere, to us or to anyone else;
  • is hidden on screen until you choose to show it, and is hidden again when you leave the app. On Android, screenshots and screen recording are blocked while My cards is open. Card numbers and CVVs you copy are cleared from the clipboard after one minute.

Anyone who can use your unlocked phone can open the app and view your saved cards, so keep a screen lock on your phone. You can edit or delete a saved card at any time.

Reading a card with NFC (Android)

On Android phones with NFC, you can fill in a card's number and expiry by holding the card against the phone. The app only reads; it does not make a payment, charge the card or change anything on it. The CVV is not stored on the card's chip, so it is always typed by you. What is read is used only to fill in the form and is saved only if you tap Save.

Travel documents and journal photos

In Documents you can keep photos of your passport, visas, tickets, bookings and similar documents, with details you type (name, number, country, expiry date and notes). The photos are copies you take with your camera or choose from your photos; they are saved in the app's private storage on your phone. They are not encrypted and are never sent anywhere by the app. They are not in your phone's own backups (Android backup is turned off for the app, and on iPhone the photo folders are excluded from iCloud and computer backups); they are included only in a backup file you make yourself in Settings (see above). The app only reads your photo library when you choose a photo, and only the photo you choose. Expiry warnings are shown inside the app and, if you allow notifications, as reminders on your phone. Photos you add to your trip journal and receipt photos you attach to expenses are kept the same way. You can export a journal as a PDF; like other exports, it goes only where you choose to send it.

Camera (QR reader)

The QR reader uses your camera only while it is open, to find QR codes. Camera images are processed on your phone and are never saved, uploaded or shared. (The camera is also used if you choose to photograph a document; that photo is saved in Documents as described above.) The app shows you what a code contains and does nothing with it until you tap an action, such as opening a link, paying with your UPI app or copying text. Those actions open other apps or websites, whose own policies apply.

Exchange rates

To show exchange rates, the app downloads public rate data, at most a few times a day, from ExchangeRate-API over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection. The request contains no personal information. As with any internet request, their servers receive standard technical information such as your IP address; ExchangeRate-API's terms cover how they handle it. We do not receive it.

Travel guides (Discover)

When you open Discover for a place (in Things to do, or from the home screen while travelling), the app downloads that place's travel guide from Wikivoyage, the free travel guide run by the Wikimedia Foundation, over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection. The request contains only the place name. As with any internet request, Wikimedia's servers receive standard technical information such as your IP address; the Wikimedia privacy policy covers how they handle it. We do not receive it. The guide is saved on your phone for offline use and fetched again after a week. "What's on this week", "Top sights on Maps" and "Reviews on Tripadvisor" open those sites in your browser or maps app, whose own policies apply.

Weather and disaster alerts

The Weather tool, and the weather card on the home screen while travel mode is on, download the forecast for the city you chose (or the travel country's capital): on iPhone from Apple Weather (Apple's WeatherKit service), and on Android from MET Norway. Only the city's map coordinates are sent, never your own location; the app does not use GPS or location services. On iPhone, Apple Weather also provides official severe weather warnings from national weather services. Current disaster alerts (earthquakes, cyclones, floods, volcanoes, wildfires, droughts) are downloaded from GDACS, the UN and European Commission's Global Disaster Alert and Coordination System; that request contains no location at all, and the app picks out alerts near your city on your phone. As with any internet request, these services receive standard technical information such as your IP address under their own policies; we do not receive it. Forecasts and alerts are kept on your phone for a short time so they show without a connection. If you allow notifications, the app tells you about a new serious alert or a strong-wind, heavy-rain or extreme-temperature warning for the city you're travelling in, while the app is running. The list of towns used to choose a city is built into the app (GeoNames, CC BY 4.0).

Exchange rates, travel guides, weather and disaster alerts are the only network requests the app makes on its own.

Backups

On Android, the app is excluded from backups entirely. On iPhone, saved cards are never backed up (see above) and photos are excluded; your other app data (such as trips, plans and your medical card) may be included in your iPhone's own device backup, which Apple encrypts and which only you can restore.

Links to other sites and apps

Tapping "Rates By ExchangeRate-API", opening a link from a QR code, or paying with a UPI app takes you to another website or app. This policy does not cover them; their own policies apply once you leave Solo Traveler.

Deleting your data

Everything the app keeps is on your phone, so you are always in control: delete a saved card in My cards, or uninstall the app or clear its storage and everything is gone. We hold nothing from the app that we could delete for you.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13, and it does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children.

Changes

If a future version of the app handles information differently, we will update this policy and the date at the top before that version is released.
